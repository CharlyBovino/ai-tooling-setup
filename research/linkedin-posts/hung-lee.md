### Topic

AI Token Economics and Organizational Costs

### Date

2026-06-08

### Link

[https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hunglee_what-if-ai-costs-more-than-humans-with-activity-7469701031484964864-wmLU](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hunglee_what-if-ai-costs-more-than-humans-with-activity-7469701031484964864-wmLU)

### Summary

Hung Lee explores the economic implications of AI adoption, arguing that decreasing token prices do not necessarily reduce organizational costs because consumption often increases faster than efficiency gains.

### Key Insights

- Lower AI costs can drive higher overall consumption.
- AI budgets require governance and operational controls.
- Engineering teams are often the largest consumers of AI resources.
- Organizations must evaluate both technological and human value creation.

### Community-Led Growth Relevance

Community leaders increasingly operate in AI-enabled environments. Understanding the economic realities of AI adoption helps organizations balance automation with human-driven community experiences.

AI Agents vs Human Labor Economics

### Link

[https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hunglee_ai-token-cost-vs-human-fte-is-the-bill-activity-7467500192032878592-Udwj](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hunglee_ai-token-cost-vs-human-fte-is-the-bill-activity-7467500192032878592-Udwj)

### Summary

Hung raises questions about whether AI-driven workflows may eventually become more expensive than expected, particularly as organizations become dependent on external providers and usage-based pricing models.

### Key Insights

- AI adoption introduces new forms of vendor dependency.
- Operational costs may become difficult to predict.
- Eliminating human expertise too early creates organizational risk.
- Cost evaluation should include flexibility and resilience.

### Community-Led Growth Relevance

Communities can serve as strategic knowledge networks that preserve expertise and adaptability. This becomes increasingly important when organizations rely heavily on external AI infrastructure.
