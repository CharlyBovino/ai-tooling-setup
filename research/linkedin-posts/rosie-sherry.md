#### Post 1

### Topic

Owned Platforms vs Platform Dependency

### Date

2026-06-08

### Link

[https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosiesherry_start-your-own-blog-your-own-email-list-activity-7469738638109638656-mlWb](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosiesherry_start-your-own-blog-your-own-email-list-activity-7469738638109638656-mlWb)

### Summary

Rosie Sherry argues that community builders should invest in assets they directly control, such as websites, newsletters, podcasts, and communities, rather than relying exclusively on social media platforms.

### Key Insights

- Social platforms are rented audiences rather than owned audiences.
- Long-term community resilience depends on owning distribution channels.
- Email lists, websites, and communities provide greater control over audience relationships.
- Sustainable growth requires reducing dependence on third-party platforms.

### Community-Led Growth Relevance

Community-Led Growth benefits from assets that remain under organizational control. Building owned channels increases resilience, improves member retention, and protects community relationships from platform changes.

### Link

[https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosiesherry_a-while-back-i-wrote-about-the-idea-that-activity-7468590474992459776-hWa-](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosiesherry_a-while-back-i-wrote-about-the-idea-that-activity-7468590474992459776-hWa-)

### Summary

Rosie proposes applying an AI MVC (Minimum Viable Community) mindset to community building, suggesting that traditional approaches often require significant effort before validating demand or engagement.

### Key Insights

- Traditional community launches often suffer from low traction.
- AI can help validate assumptions before scaling community initiatives.
- Communities should be treated as experiments during early stages.
- Rapid iteration can reduce wasted effort and improve member fit.

### Community-Led Growth Relevance

This perspective introduces a lean methodology to Community-Led Growth. Instead of building large infrastructures immediately, organizations can validate participation patterns and member needs before investing heavily in community operations.
